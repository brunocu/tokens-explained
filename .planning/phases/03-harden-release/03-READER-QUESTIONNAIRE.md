# Phase 3: Reader questionnaire

For the two non-programmers who read Post 1 ("What is a token?") alone (D-21, D-22). The answers are open and in their own words. There are no scores, no ratings and no right answers: the aim is to find where the post confuses someone who is not the author.

Reading happens **after the first deployment**, on the real public address (D-22). Release does not wait for it: fixes ship as follow-up changes.

## What to tell the reader before they start

- Please read "What is a token?" on the real public web address I send you, on the phone or computer you normally use, **alone**, without me explaining anything.
- Take your time and try the widgets if you feel like it. Nobody is testing you; if something is unclear, that is the post's fault, and that is exactly what I want to find.
- As you read, note where you stop, get lost or want to ask a question (or say it out loud if I am recording). A line like "I stopped at the part about bytes" is perfect.
- When you are done, answer the questions below in your own words. Short answers are fine. "I don't know" is a useful answer.

Expectation: two readers find the most glaring problems, not all of them (assumption A7).

## Questions

1. In your own words, what was this post about?
2. Where did you slow down, re-read something, or feel lost?
3. Was there a word you did not understand or were not sure of? (If nothing comes to mind, think about these: **token**, **vocabulary**, **byte**, **merge**.) What did you think it meant?
4. Which of the interactive parts did you try (the text box where you type, the step-by-step demo, the comparison of two tokenizers, the languages table)? What did you expect it to do before you tried it, and what did it do?
5. You may have seen some boxes in the text box with diagonal stripes (hatched). What do you think they mean? (Do not scroll back to look for an explanation first. Say what you think, then tell me if you found one.)
6. What would you tell a friend this post says, in one or two sentences?
7. Did anything seem wrong, broken or surprising? (A number that did not match, something that did not load, something that looked odd on your device.)
8. What device and browser did you read it on? (For example: "iPhone, Safari" or "Windows laptop, Chrome".)
