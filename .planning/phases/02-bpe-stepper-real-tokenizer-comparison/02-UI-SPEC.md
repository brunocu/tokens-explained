## 1. BPE Stepper Contract (island: `BpeStepper.svelte`, `client:visible`)

### Data contract the UI needs (pure TypeScript, D-04)

`trainBPE(corpus, nMerges) -> Step[]` (module under `src/core/`, Vitest-tested) must return, per step, everything the UI renders so the component only indexes into the trace:

- `pairs`: ranked list of every pair counted **before** the merge, each `{ left, right, count, sources: [{ word, times }] }`, sorted count descending, then by the tie-break order. `sources` powers the "6 in newest and 3 in widest" breakdown.
- `chosen`, `newToken` (text and recipe `left + right`), `tied`: the pairs sharing the top count (length 1 means no tie), and `tieKind`: `"letters"` (every tied pair starts with a single letter) or `"order"` (at least one tied pair starts with a merged piece).
- `words`: the splits **after** the merge, per corpus word with its count.
- `vocabulary`: the learned pieces so far, in learning order, each with its recipe.

The tie-break is the Phase 1 trainer's rule: highest count, then smallest left id, then smallest right id. It lives once, in `src/core/bpe.ts` (`trainOnPieces`, `rankPairs`, `comparePairs`); `trainMerges` in `scripts/lib/bpe-train.ts` and `trainBPE` in `src/core/stepper.ts` both call it (R-02 d, D-04). In the stepper's id space: single letters are their byte values (so "alphabetical"), the end-of-word mark sorts after the letters and is never a first piece, and merged pieces get ids in the order learned, so they sort after every letter. A golden test on the corpus plus a cross-check that runs `trainMerges` on byte pieces and compares its winners with the trace keeps stepper and trainer from disagreeing.

### Corpus and the expected trace (the golden the planner should assert; computed with the rule above on 2026-10-06)

Corpus: `low` ×5, `lower` ×2, `newest` ×6, `widest` ×3, each word written as its letters plus the end-of-word mark. Starting vocabulary: `d e i l n o r s t w §`.

| Step | Merged pair | Count | Result | Ties at the top | Words after the merge |
|------|-------------|-------|--------|-----------------|-----------------------|
| 1 | e + s | 9 | `es` | e+s, s+t, t+§ (3-way, letters) | newest and widest now contain the chip `es`; low and lower are unchanged |
| 2 | t + § | 9 | `t§` | t+§, es+t (2-way, order) | newest and widest end in the chips `es` `t§` |
| 3 | es + t§ | 9 | `est§` | none | newest and widest both end in `est§` |
| 4 | l + o | 7 | `lo` | l+o, o+w (letters) | `lo w` in low and lower |
| 5 | lo + w | 7 | `low` | none | low§ and low e r § |
| 6 | e + w | 6 | `ew` | e+w, n+e, w+est§ (3-way, letters) | `n ew est§` |
| 7 | n + ew | 6 | `new` | n+ew, ew+est§ (order) | `new est§` |
| 8 | new + est§ | 6 | `newest§` | none | low§ unmerged, `newest§` whole, `w i d est§` |

Fixed length: **8 merges (default, planner may change within 8-9)**. At 8 the reader has watched both `est§` (shared by two words) and `low` emerge, as D-02 asks. Five of the eight steps are decided by a tie, so the tie-break copy is not an edge case; it is the main thing step 2 and step 7 teach. Because the winner of step 2 is `t§` and not the paper's `est`, the post must not say the stepper reproduces the paper's merge list.

### Layout, top to bottom (one layout at every width; verified at 360, 768, 1280 px)

1. **Status header.** Label 600: "Step {k} of 8". At step 0: "Start". Static text, not a live region.
2. **Controls row** (wrapping, `gap: 8px`): `Back`, `Next` (primary, inverted style), `Run to end`, `Reset`. Native `<button>`s, `min-height: 44px`, Label size. They sit above the changing content so a reader can press Next repeatedly without scrolling.
3. **Why sentence.** `<p>` Body 18px serif, `role="status"` with `aria-live="polite"` and `aria-atomic="true"`. It is the single announcement per step (see Accessibility).
4. **Pair table** (the counts that decided merge k; hidden at step 0, replaced by an intro text in the same 240px reserved box).
5. **Words block** (the state after merge k).
6. **Vocabulary block.**
7. **Legend**, then (step 8 only) the final screen.

Cause (counts) comes before effect (words) in reading order. Because the table shows the state **before** the merge and the chips show the state **after** it, both blocks carry a label that says so:
- Table caption: "Pairs counted before merge {k}, most common first. Showing the top 6 of {N} pairs." (when N <= 6: "All {N} pairs, most common first.")
- Words block heading (Label 600): "The words after merge {k}" (step 0: "The words, split into single letters").

### Words block

- One row per corpus word, a `<ul>` of four `<li>`. Row = a Label-size line "{word} ×{count}" (Label 600 word, `×5` Muted) above a chip row (gap 4px). The label sits above the chips, not beside them, so a seven-chip row (about 241px) fits the 294px inner card width at 360px.
- Chips use the shared chip anatomy: 2px outline from the palette entry (`i mod 4` within the row), 4px radius, padding 4px 8px, `min-height: 32px`, mono 18px, `white-space: pre`, `font-variant-ligatures: none`, `flex-wrap`, 4px gap, same print rule. Chips are not focusable and have no hover behaviour.
- **Newly merged chip(s)** (every occurrence of the token made by the latest merge, in every row): the highlight outline from the Color section. The newest vocabulary entry carries the same outline and the text "new".
- **Marker rendering:** the end-of-word mark is drawn as a `.ws` glyph inside the chip text. Before it merges it is its own chip (a chip holding only `§`); after `t + §` it is part of `t§`. The words are **not** pushed through the playground's byte-based `Token`/`TokenDisplay` path: the stepper works on letters and the marker, not bytes, and a two-byte `§` would not map onto that path. The stepper renders its own chips from strings, reusing the shared chip CSS (move the chip rules to a shared stylesheet or a small `Chip.svelte`, planner's choice; do not modify `TokenDisplay.svelte` behaviour for this).
- Screen readers: the chip run is `aria-hidden="true"`; each `<li>` also contains a visually hidden sentence "{word}, seen {n} times, split as: {piece}, {piece}, end of word", rebuilt each step.

### End-of-word marker (D-06)

- Glyph: `§` (U+00A7, section sign) **(chosen by the user, replacing the researcher's original `⁀` U+2040)**. It is not `␣`, `↵`, `⇥`, and not the paper's `</w>`. Rendered in a `.ws` span (the same sans stack as the other whitespace glyphs), `--ws-color`. The post must not call it a section of anything: it is only the stepper's end-of-word mark.
- Glyph coverage gate (the same discipline as Phase 1): `§` is Latin-1, so it is in every common font and the check is a formality. The executor still confirms it renders as a visible glyph in Chrome, Firefox and Safari (iOS), and in the mono chip font. Put the single constant (`GLYPH_EOW`) beside `GLYPH_SPACE` in `src/core/display.ts`.
- Every place the glyph appears for a screen reader has a text equivalent "end of word" (visually hidden), including pair names in the table.
- Legend copy: "§ marks where a word ends. It does the job of the ␣ you saw earlier, but sits at the end of the word instead of the start."
- Why a marker at all: it is what lets the stepper show that `low` as a whole word (`low§`) differs from `low` inside `lower`. The post explains it in one sentence, not the trace.

### Pair table

A real `<table>` (natively screen-reader friendly, D-03), inside a scroll wrapper only if needed (`overflow-x: auto`, `tabindex="0"`, `role="region"`, label "Pair counts, scrollable", same pattern as `TokenTable`).

| Column | Content |
|--------|---------|
| Pair | `{left} + {right}`, mono 14px, marker glyph with hidden text "end of word" |
| Times seen | integer, tabular figures |
| Outcome | "Merged" (winner) / "Tied" (tied at the top but not chosen) / visually empty with hidden text "Not merged" |

- Header row: `<th scope="col">`, weight 600, `--bg-2`-contrast fill (the card is already `--bg-2`, so use `--bg` for the table surface and `--bg-2` for the header, as in `TokenTable`), 1px `--border` row dividers, cell padding 8px 16px.
- Rows sorted by count descending, ties in tie-break order, so the winner is always the first row.

- No colour-only cue anywhere: the winner is marked by weight, an outline and the word "Merged".

### Why sentences (D-03; plain language; the tie-break named whenever a tie decided the step)

All sentences are produced from the step data by one pure function with Vitest cases (one per template and one per step of the golden trace). `{pair}` shows the marker as `§` visually and "end of word" to assistive tech.

| Situation | Copy |
|-----------|------|
| Step 0 | "Our tiny training text has four words: low (5 times), lower (2), newest (6) and widest (3). Each word starts as single letters followed by an end-of-word mark. Press Next to count the pairs." |
| Winner, no tie | "{L} + {R} is the most common pair. It appears {n} times: {c1} in {word1} and {c2} in {word2}. So {L} and {R} are joined into one piece, {new}." (one source: "It appears {n} times, all in {word}.") |
| Tie, tied pairs all start with a single letter (`tieKind: letters`) | "{m} pairs tie for most common, with {n} each: {list}. A tie is settled alphabetically, by the first letter of each pair. {L} + {R} comes first, so it wins and becomes {new}." |
| Tie, at least one tied pair starts with a merged piece (`tieKind: order`) | "{m} pairs tie for most common, with {n} each: {list}. A tie is settled by vocabulary order: single letters come first, then merged pieces in the order they were made. {L} comes first, so {L} + {R} wins and becomes {new}." |
| Last step | the matching sentence above, then: "That was the last merge in this demo." |

If two tied pairs ever start with the same piece, the helper must settle it by the second piece with the same ordering and say so ("The first pieces match, so the second pieces decide."). This case does not occur in the 8-step trace but the rule is part of D-04, so the template exists and is unit-tested with a synthetic step.

The word "alphabetically" is used only for `tieKind: letters` (D-04). Steps 2 and 7 would be wrong under "alphabetical" (`es` sorts before `t`, but `t + §` wins), which is why the second template exists.

### Final screen (step 8, D-07)

A block below the legend, an H3 "What you just watched" (Body size, weight 600) and one paragraph:

"Every merge joined the most common pair at that moment, and each new piece stays in the vocabulary. This is how a tokenizer's vocabulary gets built. Real tokenizers do the same on far more text, with far more merges."

Plus nothing else: no input box, no "try your own word" (D-07 rejects it), no link to the next section.

### Motion (D-02, FOUND-05)

Motion-allowed readers get one CSS-only effect: the newly merged chip fades in over 200ms (`opacity` 0.4 to 1 via `@keyframes`). Reduced-motion readers get instant state changes, because the Phase 1 global `prefers-reduced-motion` block sets `animation: none !important`. **No Svelte `transition:` or `animate:` directives** (they run on the Web Animations API, which the CSS block does not reliably cancel; the CLAUDE.md note recommends them for the stepper, and this contract deliberately does not take that option so the Phase 1 reduced-motion guarantee holds without extra JavaScript). If the planner later wants `animate:flip`, it must gate the duration to 0 with `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")` and prove it in e2e.

## 2. Comparison Contract (island: `Comparison.svelte`, `client:visible`)

Layout decision (D-15): **stacked rows, one example at a time, chosen from a picker.** Rejected: columns (a 680px column cannot hold three chip columns, and 360px certainly cannot) and all five examples stacked at once (five examples times three tokenizers is about 2,500px of scrolling and no stable place to compare). One example at a time keeps the three rows in one stable block that the reader can compare by eye.

### Structure inside the card, top to bottom

1. **Picker.** Legend (Label 600): "Pick a text". Five native radio inputs styled as wrapping bordered buttons (`gap: 8px`, `min-height: 44px`), labels exactly the playground example labels (`src/core/examples.ts`, D-12): "A rare long word", "Numbers and dates", "An emoji", "A line of classic literature", "A non-English line". Default selection: the first. Arrow keys move and select (native radio behaviour). Selected option: accent fill (Color list item 3). The list is **imported from `examples.ts`**, never retyped, so the playground and the comparison cannot drift.
2. **Text.** Label 600 "Text" and the example text in mono 18px, `white-space: pre-wrap`, `dir="auto"`, in a `--bg` box with the Phase 1 input border (read-only display, not a form field).
3. **Three tokenizer rows**, always in this order: Toy, GPT-2, BERT (see "Row anatomy").
4. **One shared legend** (see "Legend").
5. **What to notice** note.
6. **Text alternative** (`<details>`).
7. **Source and date line**, then its `<details>`.
8. **Own-text block** (separated by a 1px `--border` top rule and 32px padding).

### Row anatomy (one per tokenizer)

- Header line: tokenizer name (Label 600) and "{n} tokens" (Label, Muted, tabular figures), then a one-line subline (Label, Muted) underneath. Chips below, using `TokenDisplay` with `mode="text"`, `showBytes={false}`, `legend={false}`, `emptyState={false}`, `splitCount={0}`, `compositeCount={0}` (the explanatory notes about split characters belong to the playground; the comparison shows one shared legend instead).
- Names and sublines (D-13, TOK-09: every comparison names each tokenizer and its date/version):

| Row | Name | Subline (default copy; years are the original paper years and must be fact-checked again in Phase 5) |
|-----|------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Toy | "Toy tokenizer (from the playground)" | "Small, trained on a little English text. Works on bytes." |
| GPT-2 | "GPT-2 (2019)" | "Works on bytes like the toy, with a far bigger vocabulary." |
| BERT | "BERT, uncased (2018)" | "A different method, called WordPiece. It lower-cases text, removes accents, ignores spaces, and marks a piece that continues a word with ##." |

- Rows are separated by 24px (lg); each row is a `<section>`-less `div` with an `aria-labelledby` pointing at its name, so the three read as three labelled groups. No row is collapsed or hidden.
- The row header also says whether the result is **precomputed or live** (see the own-text block); in the curated block every row is precomputed, and the source line says so.

### What the rows must display (adapter rules, D-11, FOUND-06)

The real-tokenizer adapter returns the existing `Token` shape (id, bytes, isPartial) so `TokenDisplay` is reused. UI-visible consequences the adapter and its Vitest golden cases must guarantee (cases: emoji, CJK, numbers, a leading space, a newline, a word that BERT cannot encode):

- A character split across tokens appears as the Phase 1 hatched partial chips inside a character frame, never a replacement symbol, never `Ġ`, `Ċ` or any other raw byte-mapping glyph from GPT-2's token strings (GPT-2 spaces show as `␣` like the toy). Build bytes by inverting GPT-2's byte-to-unicode table, never by `decode([id])` per token.
- BERT pieces show as the literal wordpiece: `##ing` stays `##ing`. Spaces are not part of BERT tokens, so BERT rows have no `␣` chips. Output is lower-case because the tokenizer lower-cases; the subline says so.
- BERT's unknown piece shows as a chip reading `[UNK]` (never blank, never a replacement symbol). It is a **special chip**: Phase 1's `Token` type gains one optional boolean, `special?: boolean` (default false, additive, no existing caller changes), `TokenDisplay` renders `data-special="true"` and the neutral solid style from the Color table.
- The adapter does **not** add the start and end markers some models add (`[CLS]`, `[SEP]`, a leading beginning-of-text token). Rows show only the pieces cut from the text, and counts exclude those markers. The source `<details>` says so in one sentence.
- Bytes of `[UNK]` are the UTF-8 bytes of the literal text `[UNK]`; it is never marked partial.

### Legend (one shared line under the three rows)

Built from exported string constants (one source of truth shared with `TokenDisplay`'s legend; the planner either extracts a small `TokenLegend.svelte` or exports the text constants; do not retype the sentence, and coordinate with the user's uncommitted edit to `TokenDisplay.svelte` before touching that file):

- always: "␣ space   ↵ new line   ⇥ tab   Hatched chips are raw bytes. Hatched chips in the same frame make up one character, which is shown in front of them."
- only when a displayed row contains a special chip: "[UNK] means the tokenizer had no piece for that word or character and gave up."

The `##` meaning lives in BERT's subline, so it is always visible next to the row it explains.

### What to notice (D-13, TOK-09)

A `<p>` Body 18px serif preceded by a Label 600 lead "What to notice", one or two sentences, hand-written per example, shown directly under the legend. The text swaps with the example. **Final wording is written by the author after the precompute exists (it depends on the actual splits) and checked once by eye (D-13).** The intent per example (the content writer owns the actual sentences; do not state any split that has not been read from the precomputed output):

| Example | What the note must lead the reader to see |
|---------|-------------------------------------------|
| A rare long word | The three tokenizers break the long word at different places; BERT marks continuation pieces with `##`. |
| Numbers and dates | How each tokenizer cuts digits and punctuation, and that BERT drops spaces and lower-cases. |
| An emoji | Byte-level tokenizers (toy, GPT-2) split the emoji into byte pieces; BERT cannot represent it and shows `[UNK]` if that is what the data says. |
| A line of classic literature | Common English words are mostly single pieces for the real tokenizers, while the toy splits more often because it learned from less text. |
| A non-English line | The toy and GPT-2 spend many byte pieces on characters that BERT may treat very differently; the counts differ a lot. |

The planner may add a cheap build-time test that fails if a note names a token count that disagrees with the fixture (recommended, not required by D-13).

### Own-text block ("your own text"; D-10, FOUND-05, Phase 5 criterion 4)

Heading (Label 600): "Try your own text". The block is a separate region so typing here can never change the curated rows or their notes.

**Rule that is a test (e2e `privacy.spec.ts`): no request to any `/tokenizers/` URL happens until the load button is pressed, and typing never causes a network request.** The textarea does not exist before load, so there is nothing to type into.

| Phase | What the reader sees | Copy |
|-------|----------------------|------|
| idle | Short paragraph and one button | "Want to use your own text? This downloads two small tokenizer files, GPT-2 and BERT, once (about {size} MB in total). Nothing is downloaded until you press the button." Button: "Load GPT-2 and BERT (about {size} MB)". |
| loading | Button replaced by a status line (`role="status"`, no spinner, no progress animation) and `aria-busy="true"` on the block | "Downloading the GPT-2 and BERT tokenizers (about {size} MB)..." |
| ready | One polite announcement "Loaded. Type below." (the status line is then removed instantly, no animation), then: textarea, three live rows, legend, privacy line | see below |
| error | Error-colour sentence plus a "Try again" button; success of one of the two files is kept, only the failed file is retried | "The {GPT-2 / BERT / GPT-2 and BERT} tokenizer could not be downloaded. Check your connection and try again." |
| tokenizer exception while ready | Row replaced by the Phase 1 failure box style | "The tokenizers could not run on this text. Edit the text, or reload the page. Your text stays in your browser." |

- `{size}` is **measured, not typed**: computed at build time from the gzip sizes of the two shipped files (STACK.md estimate: GPT-2 about 0.55 MB, BERT about 0.19 MB, so about 0.7 MB, matching D-10) and checked by a test so the label cannot drift from the files.
- The load button is the only trigger. Hover, focus, scroll and the picker never preload. Loading is initiated by `loadTokenizer(id)` (async, behind the adapter, so it can move to a Worker later, D-10); dynamic `import()` or `fetch()` runs inside the click handler, never at component top level.
- Ready state content: Label 600 "Your text", a `<textarea>` with the exact Phase 1 attributes (`autocapitalize="off" autocorrect="off" spellcheck="false" dir="auto" maxlength="1000"`, mono 18px, IME composition pause), prefilled with the **currently selected example's text** (so the first live result matches what the reader just saw), then three rows in the same anatomy as the curated rows (names and sublines identical, header says "{n} tokens (live)"), the shared legend, and the Phase 1 privacy line "Your text stays in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere." (`aria-describedby` on the textarea). The limit message is the Phase 1 sentence with "this box" instead of "this playground".
- No "what to notice" note and no source line for live text (there is nothing hand-written for arbitrary input); a single Label Muted line appears instead: "Counts here are computed in your browser with the downloaded files."
- One debounced (500ms) `role="status"` live region announces "Toy tokenizer: {a} tokens. GPT-2: {b} tokens. BERT: {c} tokens." Chips are never announced individually.
- The loaded block adds its own text alternative `<details>` (same three tables), summary "View your results as tables".
- Persistence: none (no `localStorage`); the HTTP cache is the only reuse.
