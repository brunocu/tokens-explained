## Re-check of the CHECK items (2026-10-06, after the first executor run)

All four CHECK rows above were re-opened (PDFs downloaded and read with pdftotext; code read from raw GitHub; abstract from the Crossref API) and are now **verified**, with these exact findings:

- slp3: chapter titles as printed on page 1 of each PDF, draft of August 19, 2026: ch. 1 "Introduction", ch. 2 "Words and Tokens", ch. 5 "Embeddings", ch. 6 "Neural Networks". Ch. 2 sec. 2.4.1: "... until k merges have been done creating k novel tokens; k is thus a parameter of the algorithm. The resulting vocabulary consists of the original set of characters plus k new symbols." Ch. 1 has no one-sentence definition of NLP; it states the goal "of getting computers to help people by performing useful tasks involving human language".
- sennrich sec. 3.2: "The final symbol vocabulary size is equal to the size of the initial vocabulary, plus the number of merge operations - the latter is the only hyperparameter of the algorithm." Abstract: "... encoding rare and unknown words as sequences of subword units."
- morfessor: title is "Unsupervised models for morpheme segmentation and morphology learning" (not "induction"), ACM TSLP vol. 4 no. 1, art. 3, Jan. 2007, doi 10.1145/1187415.1187418. The abstract speaks of "the unsupervised induction of a simple morphology from raw text data" and a "lexicon of word segments, called morphs". It does NOT mention language-specific rules or annotated data, so the article does not claim that.
- subwordnmt learn_bpe.py lines 373-374 (master, 2026-10-06): `most_frequent = max(stats, key=lambda x: (stats[x], x))`: highest count, then the pair that sorts last.
