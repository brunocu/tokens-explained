## Design facts the executor must keep

- Sample sentence: "the cat sat on the mat and the cat sat on the hat" (author may swap it later; keep it one constant).
- Pre-splitting: words are cut at spaces and the space belongs to the start of the following word, shown as the existing `␣` glyph. No piece crosses a word boundary. The `§` end-of-word mark is retired from the stepper (the "§" sentence in the post goes too).
- Base alphabet: the 256 byte values (ids 0 to 255); merge m gets id 256 + m (already how `trainOnPieces` works).
- Tie rule: `comparePairs` in src/core/bpe.ts (highest count, then smaller left id, then smaller right id). The sketch's "first seen" wording was wrong for this project. With this rule the first merges are at, he, the, then ␣the, ␣c, ␣o, ␣s, ␣on, ␣sat, ␣cat; verify from the real trace.
- Merge budget: 10 (so ␣sat and ␣cat form; confirm in the trace, otherwise pick the smallest budget where both do). Vocabulary at the end = 256 + budget.
- Stages (13 in the sketch, regenerate the count from the budget): text, bytes, starting pieces, one stage per merge, stop, unseen words. Merge stage k shows the state BEFORE merge k with every occurrence of the winning pair inside one box; Next plays a short fuse animation (pair slides together, about 0.3 s) and the next stage shows the new piece with the double border and pop-in. `prefers-reduced-motion`: no animation.
- Meaning never by colour alone: boxed pair uses a heavy outline; ties show as a hatched bar plus the words "tied"; the winner row says "Merged".
- Unseen words panel: " that" (built from known pieces) and a cat emoji (falls back to raw bytes, shown in hex). All values from `encode` over the learned merges, never typed by hand.
- Colour palette and tokens: src/styles/tokens.css only.
